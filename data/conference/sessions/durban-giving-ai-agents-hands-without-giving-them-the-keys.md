# Giving AI Agents Hands Without Giving Them the Keys

AI agents are moving beyond answering questions to interacting with APIs, accessing data, using external tools, and taking actions on behalf of users. But as their capabilities grow, so do the security risks. How do we move beyond identifying these risks to actually securing the agents we build?

Drawing on principles from AI governance, cybersecurity risk management, and secure development, this session explores how developers can translate AI risks into practical security controls.

Using a fictional AI-powered customer-support agent as our example, we'll examine threats such as prompt injection, excessive permissions, unauthorised data access, and unsafe tool execution. We'll then connect governance decisions—such as risk assessment, accountability, acceptable use, and human oversight—to engineering practices including least-privilege access, server-side authorisation, input validation, tool restrictions, security testing, and audit logging.

We'll also explore how governance frameworks such as ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework can inform the way teams manage AI risks throughout an AI system's lifecycle.

The goal is to bridge the gap between governance and implementation: helping developers understand not only why AI agents need security controls, but also where those controls belong and how to approach building safer AI-powered applications.

You don't need to be a GRC specialist to benefit. If you're building, integrating, or experimenting with AI agents, this session will give you a practical starting point for making security part of the development process.

Learning outcomes

By the end of this session, attendees will be able to:

Identify key security risks associated with AI agents and tool-connected AI applications.

Explain how AI governance and risk assessment translate into concrete developer security requirements.

Recognise essential controls for securing AI agents, including least privilege, server-side authorisation, human oversight, logging, and security testing.

Apply a simple governance-to-controls checklist when designing or reviewing an AI agent.
