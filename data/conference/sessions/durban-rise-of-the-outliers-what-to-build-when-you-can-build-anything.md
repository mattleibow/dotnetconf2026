# Rise of the Outliers: What to Build When You Can Build Anything

AI didn't kill the craft. It moved it.

Software used to need a market big enough to pay for it. Not any more. You can build almost anything now, so what do you build?

My family is neurodivergent, with a calendar full of therapy and a medicine cabinet complicated enough to need its own database. Most software gets built the way data gets cleaned: find the average, drop the outliers. We're the outliers, so I built for us. A medical journal that reads our lab reports and knows when the meds will run out. A to-do list that doesn't punish you for being late. A household assistant that can see my private records and nobody else's, because the database says no, not the prompt. And 'Boo', the app my eight-year-old son speaks through, where the most important AI decision was where to keep AI out. All of it runs on an old gaming PC.

This is four months of receipts from a family that lives in it: the evals, the bill and the failures. Why I build with Claude Code, run on OpenRouter at fractions of a cent a turn, and stay loyal to neither. Why the speech model runs in my house, picked by testing it on our own South African accents. What to do when the eval says 81% and your family says "it ignored me again". 
And live: you get my assistant's WhatsApp number and twenty minutes to get anything out of my house.

Fair warning: I'll be asking the questions. Come with a problem only you have, and leave knowing how to build for it.
